## **Visualize Challenge 3: *Which common medications make heat more dangerous, and what should prescribers and health systems do about it?*** {#visualize-challenge-3:-which-common-medications-make-heat-more-dangerous,-and-what-should-prescribers-and-health-systems-do-about-it?}



*Context:* Many widely prescribed medications, including diuretics, beta blockers, anticholinergics, antipsychotics, lithium, insulin and SGLT2 inhibitors, can impair temperature regulation or fluid balance, change how a drug is absorbed, or degrade when stored in the heat. These effects raise the risk of heat illness, hospitalization and death during heat waves, yet patients are rarely counseled and the evidence is scattered across pharmacology, epidemiology and climate research. Health systems and public health agencies around the world are starting to issue heat-health guidance, but it is uneven in what it covers and how specific it is for clinicians.

*Challenge:* Use the System Public Graph to map the evidence linking medications to heat-related outcomes and organize it into actionable buckets, for example:

* Dose adjustment, such as insulin absorbing faster in the heat

* Substitution or temporary holding, such as diuretics or anticholinergics

* Closer monitoring, such as lithium levels or hydration status

* Storage and stability, such as insulin, inhalers or epinephrine

For each bucket, show how strong the evidence is, which prescribers are most involved (primary care, psychiatry, cardiology, endocrinology, nephrology, pediatrics) and what a prescriber might reasonably change ahead of or during a heat wave. Compare this against heat-health guidance from health systems and public health agencies globally to highlight where guidance is missing or out of step with the evidence. Teams can also explore what research exists on using wearable data, such as heart rate or skin temperature, to personalize heat guidance for people on these medications, and where that evidence runs out. Mapping where in New York City heat exposure and these prescriptions overlap is an optional extension.

*What you'll produce:* A visual evidence map of heat-sensitive medications grouped by type of action, showing evidence strength, the prescribers involved and gaps in current guidance, which a clinician, pharmacist or public health official could use to plan heat-wave counseling and outreach.
