Which common medications make heat more dangerous?

17 drug classes against the four things a prescriber can do before a heat wave. Each cell shows what kind of evidence stands behind the action.

The map

Select a cell to see the draft action and the studies behind it. Orange is the only colour: a study measured the drug with heat and found a higher risk.

A dot means no heat-specific action is expected. A gap or "no clear effect" cell is not a green light: absence of evidence is not evidence of safety. Action wording is a draft for clinician and pharmacist review.

Where the indirect evidence runs, and where it stops

Each ribbon is a set of studies. Left: a drug class linked to a mechanism, in any setting. Right: heat linked to the same mechanism. A class is supported by mechanism only where both sides meet. Hover or select a class or a mechanism.

Ribbon width grows with the number of studies (square-root scale). A count shows how much was published, not how strong the link is. This is mechanism support: plausible, not measured.

Methods and limits

Before using this

  • Draft actions. The wording is for clinician and pharmacist review. It is not a protocol.
  • Mechanism support is not risk. It means the pathway is plausible, in separate studies. Nobody measured it in heat.
  • Unvetted rows are marked †. They come from the live API, where System estimates about 20% are mis-extracted.
  • Nothing is hand-checked yet. No study has been compared with its abstract.
  • Storage advice comes from drug labels. The graph has almost nothing on drug stability.
  • No evidence is not safety. A gap or a null result is not a green light.

How a cell is graded

  • Measured with heat. At least one finding pairs the drug, or its class, with heat. The cell shows whether it found a higher risk or no clear effect.
  • Mechanism support. Graded on the cell's main mechanism only. Strong: 30 or more drug studies and 10 or more heat studies. Moderate: at least 10 and 3. Weak: both present, below that.
  • Partial. The drug side exists; no study links heat to the mechanism.
  • Gap. The action is in labels or guidance. The graph has nothing for its mechanism.
  • Drug-side counts keep only findings in people where the drug is the exposure. Mechanisms are matched on variable names, so counts are indicative.
