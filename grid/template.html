Which common medications make heat more dangerous?

Review topics for 17 drug classes before and during a heat wave, with the evidence behind each one. Actions are drafts for clinician and pharmacist review. No study has been hand-checked yet.

Key findings

A dot means no heat-specific action is expected. A gap or "no clear effect" cell is not a green light: absence of evidence is not evidence of safety. Counts of studies show how much was published, not how strong the link is.

Where the indirect evidence runs, and where it stops

Each ribbon is a set of studies. Left: a drug class linked to a mechanism, in any setting. Right: heat linked to the same mechanism. A class has mechanism support only where both sides meet. Hover or select a class or a mechanism.

Ribbon width grows with the number of studies (square-root scale). Mechanism support means the pathway is plausible; nobody measured it in heat.

Methods and limits

Before using this

  • Draft actions. The wording, triggers and follow-up are for clinician and pharmacist review. They are not a protocol.
  • A forecast is not an order. Nothing here supports changing a medication on a heat forecast alone.
  • Mechanism support is not risk. The pathway is plausible, in separate studies. Nobody measured it in heat.
  • Unvetted rows are marked †. They come from the live API, where System estimates about 20% are mis-extracted.
  • Nothing is hand-checked yet. No study has been compared with its abstract.
  • Storage advice comes from drug labels. The graph has almost nothing on drug stability.

How a cell is graded

  • Measured with heat. At least one finding pairs the drug, or its class, with heat. The cell says whether it found a higher risk or no clear effect.
  • Mechanism support. Graded on the cell's main mechanism only. Strong: 30 or more drug studies and 10 or more heat studies. Moderate: at least 10 and 3. Weak: both present, below that.
  • Partial. The drug side exists; no study links heat to the mechanism.
  • Gap. The action is in labels or guidance. The graph has nothing for its mechanism.
  • Drug-side counts keep only findings in people where the drug is the exposure. Mechanisms are matched on variable names, so counts are indicative.
