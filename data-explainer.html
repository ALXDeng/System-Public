What the System Public Graph holds for medications and heat

A walk through the dataset's structure, then what it does and doesn't contain for Visualize Challenge 3. Built from the public Dataverse snapshot (v2.1, July 2026).

1. The graph is a pile of 2 million statistical findings

Each row is one number pulled out of one paper by an AI extractor: "X is associated with Y, odds ratio 1.6". Everything else in the dataset exists to label and group those rows.

2. Five tables, joined by IDs

A finding links two variables (the paper's own wording). Each variable is mapped to a concept (a Wikidata entry) so that different wordings line up. Relationships are just findings rolled up to concept pairs.

At concept level this finding becomes "antihypertensive drug → mortality rate", pooled with 67 unrelated findings from 42 other studies. For this challenge you have to work at the variable-name level, because the heat context only survives in the names.

Practical consequences: half the findings have no confidence interval, so "evidence strength" will need to lean on number of studies, sample size from the population text, and statistic type. The only study type recorded is "Experimental Trial"; everything else is blank, so trial vs observational is partly knowable and nothing finer. There is no publication year, sex, or country column.

5. But both halves of the chain are well covered

The graph is strong on what heat does to health, and on the side effects through which these drugs make heat dangerous. The two meet at shared outcomes such as kidney injury, low sodium and fainting.

This is the realistic shape of your project: a two-step evidence map, drug → physiological effect ← heat, with the graph supplying each arm and the direct drug-and-heat link shown as the gap. For example, diuretics → acute kidney injury (76 studies) and heat → acute kidney injury (14 studies) are both there; diuretics → heat illness is not.

6. Things to keep in mind when using it

  • Extraction errors. The docs say about 20% of associations returned by the API are inaccurately extracted and recommend keeping only those that also appear in the Dataverse CSV, which is what this page uses. Direction (which variable is the cause) can be swapped.
  • Concept mapping is lossy. "Heatwaves" variables are sometimes filed under the concept "heat stroke"; many drug-specific variables collapse into broad concepts. Search names, not just concepts.
  • My matching is keyword-based. The counts here come from pattern matching on variable names. They show the scale of coverage, not a curated evidence base; a careful version needs manual review of each row.
  • Absence is not evidence of absence. The well-known heat-wave case-control studies on psychotropics and diuretics exist in the literature. Their absence here means the graph missed them (results in tables are under-extracted), which is itself a finding worth showing.
  • Guidance documents are not in the graph. The comparison with CDC, WHO, UK or NYC heat-health guidance needs sources you collect yourself.
